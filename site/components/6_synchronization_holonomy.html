5. Synchronization Holonomy and the Redshift Degeneracy

If the spatial metric is static and eternal, the cosmological redshift ($z$) observed by Hubble must be generated by a non-kinematic mechanism. The TEP framework demonstrates that spatial expansion and temporal field gradients are mathematically degenerate; they produce the exact same observable data, but require fundamentally different physical assumptions.

5.1 The Mathematical Degeneracy

In the standard FLRW metric, cosmological redshift is derived as the ratio of the spatial scale factor at the time of observation to the scale factor at the time of emission:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:spatial_redshift} 1 + z = \frac{a(t_{\rm obs})}{a(t_{\rm emit})} \end{equation}

The TEP framework fixes the spatial scale factor to $a = 1$, rendering the spatial universe static. However, because the matter Lagrangian couples to the effective metric, photon frequencies are modulated by the local temporal amplification factor $\chi(\Phi_\tau, \Gamma_t)$. The exact same redshift is therefore produced by the kinetic gradient of the proper time field:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:temporal_redshift} 1 + z = \frac{\chi_{\rm obs}}{\chi_{\rm emit}} \end{equation}

This derivation proves that Hubble's data never uniquely demanded spatial expansion. The expanding metric was merely one possible solution, chosen strictly because the isochrony axiom had preemptively outlawed the alternative.

5.2 Redshift as Synchronization Holonomy

Under the TEP formulation, cosmological redshift is not a Doppler effect, nor is it the physical stretching of light waves by expanding space. It is the accumulated synchronization holonomy of a photon crossing a gradient in the proper time field.

As a photon traverses vast cosmic distances, it navigates a universe evolving "downward" along a chronometric gradient—from a state of high temporal shear in the distant past to our current, relatively flat local temporal vacuum. The frequency shift is an inescapable phase-shift resulting from this temporal topography. This elegantly validates Zwicky's 1929 instinct that redshift is a property of the intervening medium (energy loss), while eliminating his fatal flaw: because the photon is responding to the smooth scalar field gradient rather than physically scattering off dust or plasma, the images of distant galaxies remain perfectly sharp.
