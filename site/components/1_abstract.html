Abstract

For nearly a century, the standard cosmological model has interpreted extragalactic redshift as a signature of spatial expansion. This interpretation rests on an unexamined premise inherited from the technological limits of the 1920s: the isochrony axiom, the assumption that proper time advances uniformly across all cosmic epochs ($d\tau = dt$). We show that this premise was not discovered but imposed—by an era that possessed the 100-inch Hooker telescope yet had no instrument capable of measuring the flow of time itself.

Drawing on the historical record, we demonstrate that the architects of the redshift data resisted the expanding-universe reading. Edwin Hubble flagged his velocities as “apparent,” failed the 1935 surface-brightness test of expansion, and pleaded for an “unknown principle of nature”; Albert Einstein fought the Big Bang singularity in private long after his public capitulation of 1931. The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) supplies the missing principle: a bi-metric framework in which an eternal spatial metric $g_{ij}$ is permeated by a dynamical proper time field $\Phi_\tau$.

We prove that cosmological redshift is mathematically degenerate between spatial expansion ($1+z = a_{\rm obs}/a_{\rm emit}$) and the synchronization holonomy of photons crossing a gradient in the proper time field ($1+z = \chi_{\rm obs}/\chi_{\rm emit}$). Reassigning the dynamics to the temporal sector dissolves the JWST galaxy-assembly crisis, the $H_0$ tension, the dark matter and dark energy problems, and the singularity problem—without abandoning the classical, deterministic continuum Einstein defended. The same synchronization holonomies are measurable today in terrestrial chronometry: pulsar spin-down residuals and 25-year GNSS precise clock products.
