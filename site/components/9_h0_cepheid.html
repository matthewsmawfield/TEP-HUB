8. The Hubble Tension and the Cepheid Bias

The "Hubble Tension"—the irreconcilable discrepancy between the expansion rate ($H_0$) measured via the local distance ladder (supernovae, Cepheids) and the rate inferred from the early universe (Cosmic Microwave Background)—has fractured modern cosmology. The TEP framework demonstrates that this tension is not a physical crisis, but an inevitable calibration error stemming directly from the isochrony axiom.

8.1 The Observable Response Coefficient ($\kappa$)

As explored rigorously in 18-TEP-HC, standard candles such as Cepheid variables are not perfectly rigid chronometers. Their luminosity and pulsation periods are fundamentally modulated by the local temporal topology in which they reside. Because they exist in galactic environments subject to varying degrees of Temporal Shear ($\Gamma_t$), their physical outputs are biased by an Observable Response Coefficient ($\kappa \approx 9.6 \times 10^5$ mag).

8.2 Miscalibrating the X-Axis

The entire local distance ladder is built on the assumption that a Cepheid in a distant, high-shear galaxy ticks and burns exactly identically to a Cepheid in our local temporal vacuum. By ignoring the $\Phi_\tau$ gradient, astrophysicists have systematically warped the X-axis (distance) of their measurements.

When the Cepheid data is properly corrected for local temporal shear, the $H_0$ tension dissolves. The discrepancy between the local universe and the early universe is simply the uncorrected measurement of the chronometric gradient between two different cosmic epochs.
